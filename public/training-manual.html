Getting Around

The three things that orient you no matter where you are: your daily dashboard, the feature finder, and the menu.

Part 1 · Screen 1

HOME — the Command Dashboard

Where it is: the top item of the menu, pinned on every screen. It's also where the office app opens.

HOME answers one question fast: how is the business doing right now? It's a read-only command board — every tile is a live number that links straight to the screen where you act on it. A manager can run the whole morning review from here without changing a single record.

A.Set what you're looking at

  1. Check the Viewing-Organization pill (top-left).

    It shows which company you're looking at. Platform owners can click it to switch to any client org — the whole board reloads for that company and your choice is remembered.

  2. Set the Period (top-right).

    This month (default), Last 30 days, This quarter, or Year to date. Every date-based tile re-queries the instant you change it.

  3. Know the four point-in-time tiles.

    Recurring revenue and Outstanding estimates (both "as of now"), Payroll % of sales (always last 6 months), and Unusual fuel / mileage flags (current) do not follow the period — they're always live. Everything else obeys the Period.

B.Read the vital signs

The tiles are the business's vital signs, each drawn as the right visual for its data — a single number, a gauge against a goal, a ranked bar chart, or a flags list.

  • Money: Sales (invoiced revenue), Recurring revenue (agreement run-rate/month), Outstanding estimates (unsold pipeline).
  • Work: Jobs / tech / day (completed jobs per tech per working day); Gross margin (a gauge with a 60% floor line); On-time arrival (a gauge with a 90% floor — within 15 min of schedule).
  • Mix & payroll: Revenue by tech and Revenue by job type (ranked bars); Payroll % of sales (a column chart against a 20% ceiling); Estimates presented vs. sold (conversion).
  • Inventory & fleet: Inventory variance $ by truck holder; Unusual fuel / mileage flags.

C.Act on a number

  1. Click a tile to drill in.

    Sales and Gross margin open Invoices; Outstanding and Presented-vs-sold open Estimates; Jobs/tech/day and On-time open Jobs Management; Recurring opens Maintenance Agreements; Payroll opens Payroll. A small ↗ marks a tile that drills.

  2. Click a single bar to pre-filter.

    On Revenue by tech or by job type, clicking one bar opens Jobs filtered to exactly that technician or job type.

  3. Ask the AI what a number means.

    Click the ✦ Investigate icon on any tile for a plain-English read of that number for this org and period — what it's saying, likely drivers, and what to do next.

D.Make the board your own (managers/owners)

These controls appear only for a super-admin or anyone with the "customize dashboard" permission; everyone else sees the board read-only.

  1. + Add KPI.

    Pick a base measure (Revenue, Gross profit, Estimate value, Jobs completed) and a breakdown (total, by tech, by job type, by customer, by month, by status). The builder picks the right chart and adds the tile to the end of the board.

  2. Arrange.

    Drag any tile by its handle to reorder; use the − S/M/L + control to resize (single-value tiles cap at M, charts go to L). Changes save automatically — click Done arranging when finished.

  3. Remove or reset.

    Remove a tile with its ×. "Reset to default" restores the standard 12-tile board (it only appears once an org has customized). The factory default can never be lost.

Scope

Layouts are saved per organization, so each client company keeps its own board, and it refetches fresh data whenever you change org, period, or a tile.

Find a Feature

Where it is: the 🧭 Find a Feature button at the top-right of every office screen.

Journey is a big app, and no one memorizes where everything lives. The Feature Directory is a searchable A–Z index of every feature, each with how to reach it from Home and a direct link. It's the fastest way to jump somewhere — or to find something when you're still learning the layout.

A.Jump to anything

  1. Click 🧭 Find a Feature.

    It's top-right on every screen, so it's always one click away.

  2. Type what you're after.

    Search filters by feature name, its navigation heading, or the page — e.g. "invoice", "payroll", "fleet".

  3. Read the row and open it.

    Each row shows the Feature, its Navigation heading and page (how to reach it from Home), and an open → link that takes you straight there.

Scope

The directory lists only features your role and your company's modules include — so what you see is what you can actually open. It's built from the live menu, so it's always current.

Part 1 · The menu

The menu & moving between apps

The left menu is a single column. HOME stays pinned at the top on every screen — tap it to jump back to the top level.

  1. Open a section.

    Tap a section — Train Station, Inventory Central, WorkForce, Command Center, Financials, or Admin — and its menu opens in place, with a "BACK TO:" line just under HOME to step back one level at a time.

  2. Switch to the Field App.

    Owners and admins see a "Field App" button near the bottom of the menu that opens the phone/field app on this device. From inside the field app, Mobile App Settings → "Switch to Office App" brings you back.

  3. Work office + field at once.

    You can be signed in on one computer and one phone at the same time — your desktop stays in the office while your phone is in the field app.

Good to know

The menu only shows sections and pages your role and your company's modules include. If something in this manual isn't in your menu, your company either hasn't enabled that module or your role doesn't include it — ask your administrator.

Part 2 · The hub

The Train Station

Where it is: the screen that opens when you sign in to the office app. Tap HOME at the top of the menu to return here from anywhere.

The Train Station is your morning starting line. Instead of checking Dispatch, then Jobs, then Maintenance, then Permitting one by one, this one screen gathers every open item across the office into a single board — and each item is a door straight to the screen where you clear it.

A.Run your morning from this screen

  1. Read the banner first.

    The colored bar at the top is your headline for the day: how many things need a hand and across how many areas — or that you're all caught up. Start here.

  2. Scan the "Needs a hand" cards.

    Each card is one area with work waiting and a count. Glance down the counts to see where the pressure is.

  3. Open the busiest card and clear it.

    Click a card to jump to the exact screen that handles that work, clear what you can, then come back to HOME.

  4. Watch it move to "Handled."

    As you clear work the count drops; at zero the card leaves "Needs a hand" and settles into "Handled" below.

  5. Finish when the banner turns green.

    "You're all caught up" means nothing is waiting on you. That's the whole job of this screen.

    The pill shows how many items wait; the line tells you what they are.

  3. Clear the work, then return to HOME.

    When you come back, the count reflects what you did.

How the counts stay honest

The counts are live and shared with the screens behind them — clearing an item in Dispatch or Jobs lowers the Train Station count by the same amount, so the two never disagree.

D.The "Handled" chips

  1. Trust them as "done for now."

    A green chip means that area is at zero — no need to open it.

  2. Click one to look anyway.

    A chip still opens its area.

  3. Watch for one jumping back up.

    The moment work appears, its chip becomes a "Needs a hand" card at the top — you never miss something new.

E.Edit — tailor the board to your company

  1. Click Edit (top-right).

    An Alerts / Off switch appears on each area; the button becomes Done.

  2. Set each area.

    Alerts — watch it and show its work here. Off — another team owns it; it drops off the board (still reachable from the menu).

  3. Click Done.

    Choices save automatically and apply to this company for everyone.

Tip

For a brand-new office, turn Off the areas they aren't running yet so the Station only shows work they own. Flip to Alerts the day they take it on.

Part 2 · Used everywhere

The + Add quick-create

Where it is: a + Add button at the top of many screens — Jobs, Jobs Management, Customers, Properties, the Calendar, the Dispatch Call Console, Estimates, Invoices, and each Project page. Wherever you see it, it works the same way, so it's explained once here.

+ Add is how you create a record without stopping what you're doing. It opens a single modal that walks you through the whole thing — and, crucially, it lets you create the customer and property inside the same form if they don't exist yet, so a brand-new caller becomes a booked job in one pass.

A.Add a Job

  1. Choose the customer.

    Search and pick an existing customer, or switch to New customer and enter their name/company, phones, emails, and notes right here. (If a customer is flagged banned, the form warns you; only an admin can override.)

  2. Choose the property.

    Pick one of that customer's properties, or add a new property (address, unit, city, county, ZIP, gate code, tenants) without leaving the form.

  3. Set the visit.

    Date, start time, and duration. Leave the date off to create an unscheduled job that lands in Dispatch.

  4. Pick the Job Type.

    This is the important choice — the job type decides how the job behaves: what fills its Diagnosis step, the estimate format, the pricebook it uses, whether it runs as a Project, and its checklist. (See Data Station → Checklists & job types.)

  5. Add the complaint, trip charge, and technicians.

    Enter the service complaint, set the trip charge (location / access / hours, which drives pricebook lookups), and assign up to four technicians.

  6. Set any guard rails.

    Mark "diagnose only" with a dollar limit to cap what can be billed without a new approval, flag "read notes before the visit," and add private (internal) notes the customer never sees.

  7. Attach to a Project if prompted.

    If the job type runs as a Project (progress billing), a Project picker appears — choose the project this job belongs to. Started from a Project page, it's linked for you automatically.

  8. Save.

    The job is created and, if scheduled, appears on the Calendar and Dispatch; if not, it waits in Dispatch's Needs-a-tech tray.

B.Add a return visit (Continue a Job)

  1. Search the existing job.

    Find it by job number or customer.

  2. Add the new segment.

    Give the return visit its own date, time, job type, technicians, and trip charge. It becomes the next segment on the same job number, so the history stays together.

C.Add a Customer or a Property on their own

  1. Customer.

    Name or company, up to two phones and two emails, acquire date, and notes.

  2. Property.

    Address and unit, city/county/state/ZIP, gate code, up to two tenants with their relationship, the bill-to customer, and notes.

+ Add — the point

Because the customer and property can be created inside the job form, you never have to leave a call to go set up records first. One form turns a phone call into a scheduled job.

Why it works this way

The job type — not free typing — decides diagnosis, pricing, and workflow, so two people adding the same kind of job get the same guard rails every time. That's how Journey keeps a growing team consistent.

Part 2 · Station

Jobs & Customers

Where it is: Train Station → Jobs & Customers. Its dashboard opens the station; the pages below sit under it.

This is the station where work becomes money and where every customer and property record lives. Start at its dashboard to see what's waiting, then use the individual pages to do the work.

1.The Jobs & Customers dashboard

The work-to-money board. It tracks jobs from completed → billed → sent → paid, and shows owners the dollars behind those tasks. Like the Train Station, the tiles themselves are the feature: each is a live count of a pipeline step that still needs a hand, and clicking it opens the exact list.

Work the tiles

  1. Read each task tile.

    Completed — needs invoicing; Incomplete jobs; Invoices to send; Unpaid invoices; Estimates to send; Estimates / System estimates to convert; Estimates out (awaiting decision).

  2. Click a tile to open its list.

    Each tile opens exactly the filtered list behind it, so you act where the work is.

  3. Switch Office ↔ Owner view.

    The "Viewing as" toggle (top-right) flips between office tasks and owner dollars — Owner adds A/R outstanding, Collected (30 days), and Won (30 days). Same data, framed for who's reading.

+ Add

This dashboard carries the + Add quick-create (see the reference above) — start a new job, customer, estimate, or invoice without leaving the board.

2.Jobs — the master list of all work

Every service call, install, and maintenance visit. Day-to-day scheduling is easier on the Calendar; Jobs is for finding and editing the record itself.

Find a job

  1. Filter by status.

    Multi-select status, with a one-click shortcut to hide completed & canceled.

  2. Search or arrive pre-filtered.

    Search matches job number, address, customer, issue, or technician. Clicking a bar on a HOME chart lands you here already filtered to that tech or job type.

  3. Set your columns.

    Show/hide fields with Columns (remembered in your browser). Job #, Segment, Date, and Customer stay pinned as you scroll sideways; click a header to sort.

Edit and act on a job

  1. Click Edit to change it inline.

    Property, date, start time, duration, job type, complaint, status, notes, and the On-My-Way / Arrival / Completed timestamps; set the trip charge and mark Diagnose-only or an authorization limit; add/remove technicians (first listed is lead ★).

  2. Open its paperwork.

    Invoice / Estimate / System Estimate buttons open or start that document. The Invoice Status column reads No invoice / Not sent / Not paid / Paid and links straight to it. A ⏳ on the date means a placeholder from an approved estimate — it still needs real scheduling.

  3. Delete, restore, export (admins).

    Delete asks for a reason and soft-deletes (linked estimates and unsent drafts go with it, nothing is erased); View Deleted Jobs restores. Export CSV downloads the filtered list.

+ Add

+ Add here creates a new job (or customer/property inside it) — the full flow in the reference above.

Job types drive the rest

A Retrofit (new-system install) auto-creates a Warranty Registration with a 30-day clock; a Preventive Maintenance job generates the PM checklist(s) for that property's systems. The job type is doing a lot of quiet work — pick it correctly.

3.Jobs Management — the parts & follow-up desk

When a job needs a part before it can be finished, it lands here (the Incomplete Jobs list). Work each row left to right. Red = nothing done yet; yellow = a return visit already exists. A row clears itself once the return visit is marked Complete.

  1. Open the follow-up estimate.

    "Open Estimate" shows it in a side panel while you fill in the parts fields. Brand / Model # / Serial # auto-fill from the property's equipment — edit if needed.

  2. Classify and record approval.

    Choose Warranty / Cash / Punchlist; Claim Status then offers only the valid choices. Record Verbal Approval (amount + Pending / Approved / Declined / Punchlist). A Declined estimate exposes "Flip to Complete" to close the job out.

  3. Order the part (+ Add Part).

    The Vendor list is filtered to suppliers that carry the equipment's brand (★ = your preferred vendor). PO # auto-assigns from the shared purchase-order sequence — leave it blank for the next number. Set part, quantity, the segment it's for, and Expected Delivery.

  4. Stage the return visit (+ New Segment).

    Creates the next segment with no date/tech and drops it into the Calendar's "Needs Dispatch" tray. When the part arrives, Mark Verified flags the segment "Parts in — ready to schedule" on its Dispatch card.

+ New

The + New button here scopes to New Job, New Segment, New Invoice, New Task, and New To-Do Item.

4.Tasks — field errands

Standalone errands for a field user (most often a parts pickup) — each becomes a time-tracked Job Card on their phone. Not the office sticky-note list (that's the To-Do list behind Dispatch's "To-Dos" tile).

  1. Click + New Task and assign it.

    Choose who it's for.

  2. Set the destination and time.

    Give it a destination, address, and time. A task can't be double-booked over that person's jobs, and it records their time and location as they run it — so an errand is scheduled, routed, and paid much like a job.

5.Customers — your directory and the customer file

  1. Find and tailor the list.

    Search by name, company, phone, or email; Show archived; Columns (remembered); Export CSV (includes status and Do-Not-Service flag).

  2. Edit a customer inline.

    Display name (First+Last / Company quick-fill), company, names, two phones, two emails, acquired date, notes. Archive/Reactivate retires or restores.

  3. Fire a customer when you must (admins).

    "Fire Customer" with a reason sets a Do-Not-Service flag that blocks new scheduling everywhere until an admin uses Lift Ban.

  4. Open the customer file.

    Click a name for the whole relationship on one screen: properties (with equipment and warranty), maintenance agreement, Contacts & Invoice Routing (who's billed / who approves — key for commercial), job history, invoices & estimates, permits, service reports, warranties, photos. An ✨ AI: summarize this customer helper drafts a plain-English account summary you can review and use.

+ Add

+ Add creates a new customer here (or a full job built around them).

6.Properties — locations, equipment & filters

  1. Find and flag.

    Search by address, customer, city, state, zip, or county. Toggle Show archived, Needs filters, or Needs mfg date; row badges flag each. Columns (remembered) and Export CSV as elsewhere.

  2. Edit the property.

    Set the customer and, when billing differs, a separate Bill To customer. Update address, gate code, up to two tenants, and notes. Archive/Reactivate as needed.

  3. Manage equipment.

    Click Equipment → add a system (outdoor/indoor/furnace brand, model, serial + install date or mfg year/month). Journey computes parts, labor, and refrigerant warranty status, reading many serials automatically. Confirm any "No mfg date" systems so warranty answers are accurate. When a system is replaced, Retire the old one (kept 90 days for size-for-size compliance, then auto-clears; Recall restores it).

  4. Record filters.

    Click Filters → record each size (W × H × thickness), MERV, location, and quantity. This is the same list techs fill in and the customer portal reads — record it once and every future filter order is two clicks.

+ Add

+ Add creates a new property (and can attach tenants and a bill-to) without leaving the page.

7.Job Estimates — repair & service quotes

Quotes for service/repair work tied to a job, tracked from sent to sold. (New-system quotes are on System Estimates.)

  1. Find and read.

    Search by estimate #, job #, or customer; filter by Approval Status (with a Hide completed & declined shortcut); Show archived. Columns show line items, the money breakdown, Projected Profit and %, the estimating tech, and status.

  2. Work an estimate.

    Set the Estimating Technician and Approval Status in the row. Send / Resend emails it (tooltip shows last-sent); View opens the customer copy; Edit opens it to change.

  3. Draft the scope with AI.

    Inside the estimate (Edit), ✨ AI: draft scope turns the line items into a short, plain-language scope-of-work summary for the customer — review, edit, and paste. It never sends on its own and never states a total.

  4. Convert an approved estimate.

    When status is Approved, a → Service Call button appears: choose a new segment of the existing job or a brand-new job. Journey creates the unscheduled repair call (no trip charge — the estimate already priced it), linked back, ready to schedule from Jobs.

8.System Estimates — new-equipment proposals

New-system quotes made against a property before any job exists — your install sales pipeline.

  1. Create a proposal (+ New System Estimate).

    It quotes a system against a property (no job needed yet).

  2. Build and manage it.

    Set the estimating tech and status inline; Send / Resend, View, Edit as on Job Estimates. In the editor, add the system from your Residential Systems book (equipment, what the install includes, warranty) plus special features and misc items.

  3. Draft the proposal with AI.

    In the editor, ✨ AI: draft proposal turns the equipment / installation / warranty lines into a warm, homeowner-friendly summary (model numbers become plain benefits) — review, edit, paste. Nothing is sent automatically; no total is stated.

  4. Convert to a job.

    Convert to Job is grayed until Approved, red when ready, blue once built. Converting creates an unscheduled Install/Retrofit job in the Calendar's Needs Dispatch tray and copies the line items onto its invoice.

9.Invoices — bill and get paid

  1. Find and read.

    Filter All / Unpaid / Paid; search by invoice #, job #, or customer; Show archived. A completed job whose invoice is still unpaid is flagged yellow. Columns, Export CSV, and a click-to-edit Diagnosis (which updates the job everywhere).

  2. Send and collect.

    Send / Resend emails the invoice with a pay link; View opens the customer copy; Edit changes it. For a sent, overdue invoice, AI reminder drafts a courteous payment reminder to review and send.

  3. Draft the work summary with AI.

    Inside the invoice (Edit), ✨ AI: draft work summary turns the line items into a plain-language recap of the work performed — review, edit, paste into the invoice or a thank-you note. Never auto-sends; never states a total.

  4. Record payment.

    Mark Paid → Record Payment: enter the actual amount, method, check number, and note. It posts to the payment ledger and settles the balance. Receive Payment (top) records a payment not tied to one row. Unmark Paid reverses with an offsetting ledger entry, keeping the audit trail.

  5. Archive or void.

    Archive hides but keeps it. Void (admins, reason required) removes it so it stops counting and its job can be deleted — it can't be undone and does not refund the customer.

+ Add

+ Add is available here to start a new invoice (or job).

10.Projects — progress billing for big contracts

Projects group the jobs of one large, multi-visit contract under a single contract total and bill it in stages (draws) instead of one invoice at the end.

  1. Set up the project.

    Open (or create) a project and set its name, Contract Total, Retainage %, and billing mode. Jobs of a project type attach automatically; you can also attach/detach jobs. The rollup tiles show Contract, Estimated, Billed, Paid, Remaining, and Retainage held.

  2. Build the draw schedule.

    Add each draw with a label and a percent of the contract or a dollar amount (a deposit, a start draw, a completion draw…).

  3. Bill a draw.

    Press Bill on a draw to generate a normal INV-#### invoice for that amount net of retainage, with a line item describing the draw. It collects and pays like any other invoice.

  4. Release retainage.

    When the job is accepted, Release retainage bills the held amount as its own draw invoice.

+ Add (on each project page)

Each individual project page has its own + Add at the top-right to create a job already linked to that project — a convenience so you don't create the job elsewhere and attach it after.

11.Payments to Confirm — the install-scheduling gate

Big-ticket installs shouldn't be scheduled until the money is settled. This page lists invoices waiting on a confirmed payment method or financing decision, so nothing gets put on the calendar prematurely.

  1. Read the state badge.

    Each row shows where it stands: Awaiting method, Financing — applied / approved / denied / funded, or a confirmed method.

  2. Confirm the method.

    When the customer's payment method is settled, Confirm it — that clears the job to be scheduled.

  3. Approve or deny financing.

    For a financing row, mark it Approved (install can be scheduled) or Denied.

  4. Mark paid when the money is in.

    Mark Paid records the payment against the invoice and settles the balance.

Why it works this way

Holding installs until payment is confirmed protects the business from scheduling crews and ordering equipment against money that never arrives — one of Journey's built-in guard rails.

Part 2 · Station

Dispatch Station

Where it is: Train Station → Dispatch Station.

Dispatch is your front-of-house: everything coming in (new requests, calls to return) and everything going out (jobs to schedule, techs to assign, filters to fulfill). You run the phones, the requests, and the board from here.

1.The Dispatch dashboard

Like the Train Station, the tiles are the feature — eight live counts of front-of-house work, each opening the exact worklist. It reads the same shared signals as the Train Station roll-up, so clearing something here clears it there too. The goal is to clear the board to zero.

  1. Read the banner + Dispatch briefing.

    Amber = work waiting (with a count); green = clear. The Quincy "Dispatch briefing" beside it gives quick context.

  2. Work the eight tiles, red first.

    New service requests → Service Requests; Jobs to schedule → Calendar; Needs dispatch (no tech) → Dispatch Map; Parts in — schedule return → Calendar; Filter orders to fulfill → Filter Orders; To-Dos → the To-Do list; Calls to return (red) → Call Log; Call follow-ups → Call Log. A missed call-back is a lost customer — handle red first.

  3. Clear and watch it drop.

    Click a tile, clear its items, and the count falls on the next load; a tile at zero moves to the green "Handled" row (still clickable).

2.Call Console — open it when the phone rings

Type the caller's number and Journey instantly identifies who's calling and pulls up everything you need — then you log the call so nothing is dropped.

  1. Pull up the caller.

Permitting Station

Where it is: Train Station → Permitting Station.

Permitting tracks every new-system install through the whole permit lifecycle — from an approved system estimate, to applying for and receiving the permit, to passing final inspection. Each install becomes a permit "package" you work step by step.

1.The Permits board

  1. Start or resume a package.

    "Approved — start the permit" lists approved system estimates not yet begun — Next Step → creates the package (System 1 seeded from the estimate's equipment). "In progress" → Resume. "Awaiting inspection" → Schedule inspection (a failed inspection is tagged red to reschedule). The table also shows each install's invoice and whether it's paid.

The 9-step permit workflow (per package)

  1. Order equipment / verify availability.

    Record the vendor, models, cost, and AHRI; optionally place the PO.

  2. Job.

    Link/confirm the install job and date.

  3. AHRI verification.

    Enter the AHRI number and upload the certificate (AHRI Directory link provided).

  4. Property card.

    Pull parcel and legal description from the county appraiser.

  5. Apply for permit.

    Open the authority's form or download the blank, and upload the completed application.

  6. Notice of Commencement.

    Required when the job total meets your NOC threshold or the authority requires it; upload each system's notarized NOC (tracked per system) and send to the recorder.

  7. Receive & record permit.

    Enter the permit number and date and upload the permit.

  8. Print & send.

    Email the package to the authority and copy the permit/AHRI/NOC to the permanent customer & property record.

  9. Inspections.

    Once the install is complete, schedule the inspection, then mark each Pass or Fail; passing all of a package's permits closes it out.

Good to know

Later steps stay locked until the earlier ones are done, and the workflow only moves forward. A finished package drops to "Completed" and shows on the customer's profile under Permits. The authorities, counties, and NOC threshold that drive these steps come from Building Authorities.

2.Building Authorities — the reference data permits run on

  1. Set permit settings & counties.

    Set the NOC threshold (the dollar amount at/above which a Notice of Commencement is required — Florida default $15,000); add each county with its property-appraiser URL and recorder vendor.

  2. Add each authority (+ Add authority).

    Contact info, county, online-application link and/or uploaded blank PDF, inspection scheduling URL/phone, and whether it Requires Notice of Commencement. Each card offers quick links (Website, Online form, NOC form, Blank PDF), Edit, and Archive/Restore.

Why keep this current

These records auto-populate the permit steps — application links and forms (Step 5), the NOC rule and recorder (Step 6), inspection scheduling (Step 9), and the email the finished package is sent to (Step 8).

  2. Fill the equipment details.

  3. Register and record the date.

608 Refrigeration Compliance

Where it is: Train Station → 608 Refrigeration Compliance.

This station keeps you square with EPA Section 608 and the AIM Act — every pound of refrigerant accounted for, every covered system watched for leaks, and every cylinder tracked cradle-to-grave.

The two rule layers

Section 608 applies to ALL refrigerant work — certified techs only, no venting, recover before opening a system, keep records. The AIM Act leak-repair rules (leak-rate thresholds + the 30-day repair clock) apply only to COVERED systems: 15 lb or more of refrigerant and not a residential/light-commercial AC or heat pump. A system's sector decides which it is.

1.Refrigerant Dashboard

  1. Read the compliance headline.

    KPI tiles: Over leak threshold (→ Systems), Added and Recovered in the last 90 days (→ Usage Log), Cylinders on hand with total pounds (→ Cylinders). The Quincy briefing leads with any repair deadlines, then cylinders to ship, then the added-vs-recovered balance.

  2. Act on anything over threshold.

    A red panel lists each covered system past its leak limit with a "Record a repair →" link to the Usage Log.

2.Refrigerant Systems — profile each system

  1. Set the profile.

    Edit a system → Refrigerant, Full charge (lb), and Sector (Residential/light-commercial, Comfort cooling, Commercial refrigeration, Industrial process). Systems come from each property's equipment — you profile them here, you don't add/retire them.

  2. Know when a system is covered.

    COVERED (leak rules apply) when full charge ≥ 15 lb AND sector is not residential/light-commercial; everything else is Exempt (a simple log, no 30-day clock). The status column shows Covered / Exempt / "Over T% · repair" plus the estimated annual leak rate.

The thresholds

Once covered, the repair limit depends on sector: 10% comfort cooling, 20% commercial refrigeration, 30% industrial process. Set the sector right and the dashboard, log, and 30-day clock all follow.

3.Refrigerant Usage Log — the record it all builds on

  1. Record an event (+ Record event).

    Date and System (auto-fills its property and refrigerant), Technician and cert type, pounds Added and/or Recovered, and optionally the Cylinder.

  2. Give it a reason.

    Top-off / leak add, repair (post-fix charge), new install charge, recovery, retirement/disposal recovery, or other; add notes (leak location, repair made). To close a leak flagged on the dashboard, log a "Repair (post-fix charge)" event.

  3. Mind the cert warning.

    The tech's EPA cert is checked against HR — a warning shows if none is on file or it's expired. It's a reminder, not a hard stop; Section 608 requires a certified tech, so heed it.

  4. Pick the right cylinder.

    Choosing a cylinder moves refrigerant in/out of it automatically — the log doesn't verify the cylinder's type matches, so pick carefully. Export CSV for your records (keep at least three years).

4.Refrigerant Cylinders — cradle to grave

  1. Add a cylinder (+ Add cylinder).

    Kind (Virgin purchased / Recovered), refrigerant, size, on-hand pounds, vendor, acquired date, notes (cylinder ID/serial). On-hand pounds then move on their own from the Usage Log.

  2. Send out a full recovery cylinder.

    "Send out" → Certified reclaimer or Certified disposal, recipient facility, and a document reference (manifest/ticket/invoice #), then Record shipment — it captures pounds shipped, zeroes the cylinder, and closes the chain.

  3. Audit the closed ones.

    "Show cylinders sent to reclaim / disposal" pulls the closed-cylinder history. Recovered cylinders sitting on hand show on the dashboard as "awaiting reclaim" so nothing lingers.

6.Replenishment

  1. Refill trucks in one click.

    Pick the warehouse to refill from; each truck shortfall gets a Transfer button that moves the suggested quantity. Warehouse shortfalls are flagged to purchase instead.

  2. Let AI prioritize.

    ✨ AI: prioritize restocking gives a short Quincy plan of which trucks and items to restock first, and anything unusual.

7.Purchase Orders

  1. Read the one list.

    Every PO: number, Designation (Replenishment, or a Job #·Segment from Jobs Management), vendor, date, status, received count, value. Job-part rows are owned by Jobs Management (Open in Jobs to verify delivery).

  2. Create a PO.

    Pick the vendor (required) and deliver-to location, add parts (search the catalog, or type a new part and create it on the spot; + New Vendor if needed). "Pull low items" auto-fills everything below reorder point at that location.

  3. Email and receive.

    Email to vendor sends it to their email on file (marked Emailed). When goods arrive, open the PO and receive against it — on-hand and costs update, and the PO advances to Partial or Received.

Numbering

POs auto-number (PO-1001, PO-1002…); moving from another system, set the next number so your sequence continues. Job POs from Jobs Management draw the same sequence, so numbers never collide.

8.Special Orders

  1. Track a per-job part.

    Create one, link the customer (or type a name), describe the part, add estimated cost, PO reference, needed-by date, and a vendor (required — + New Vendor for a one-time supplier).

  2. Move it along.

    Requested → Ordered → Received → Ready → Closed (each timestamped). Overdue past needed-by is flagged. Marking "received" never touches on-hand — the part goes straight to the job.

9.Vendor Invoices (A/P)

  1. Work the Quincy inbox.

    Invoices emailed to your intake address arrive already read and extracted. Press Review to open one pre-filled (vendor, PO, and catalog lines matched), confirm, and save into A/P — or Dismiss if it isn't a bill. You can also capture one yourself (photo/PDF) and Quincy reads it the same way.

  2. Check the 3-way match.

    It compares ordered vs received vs billed on each line and flags price/quantity variances. Stage for payment when good, or put On hold if something's off; from a matched bill you can receive the goods into stock.

It learns

Every confirmed line teaches the Vendor Cross-Reference, so the next bill from that vendor auto-matches the same part by its SKU.

10.Vendors

  1. Keep each supplier's record.

    Name, order email (so POs can be emailed), and the brands they carry — with a ★ preferred vendor per brand that Jobs Management and Replenishment use when ordering.

11.Vendor Cross-Reference

  1. Suggest matches from history.

    Pick a vendor and let Quincy propose which of their past parts map to your catalog items (it sorts single vs dual capacitors, exact sizes, accessories). Review the pre-selected picks, override or clear any, and save — nothing is stored until you confirm.

  2. Verify what Quincy learned.

    Confirmed invoices add matches automatically; those land in the "Matches to verify" queue (also counted on the dashboard). Verify, Fix, or Remove each. Matches you approve directly here are already verified.

12.Record Parts Used

The single most valuable inventory habit — it depletes stock and powers Parts Usage, Job Costing, and Demand Forecast.

  1. Pick an invoice and confirm what moved.

    Filter by recorded/not-recorded. The parts are seeded from the kits of the services billed — confirm the ones that really left the truck and record them. It depletes that tech's truck and marks the invoice recorded. (The same panel runs in the field on the mobile work order.)

13.Inventory Health (Anomalies)

  1. Scan the flags.

    A live scan for problems worth acting on: negative stock, stockouts, cost outliers, usage spikes, shrinkage, dead stock, missing cost. Grouped by type and ranked by severity, each with what it means and what to do.

  2. Fix and refresh.

    It changes nothing itself — fix the underlying issue, then Refresh to confirm the flag cleared.

14.Inventory Settings

  1. Enable the module.

    When on, invoiced pricebook parts deduct from the assigned technician's truck. Leave it off until items, trucks, and service mappings are ready.

  2. Set restock defaults.

    Weekly truck restock day(s), plus lead-time and safety-stock defaults that drive Replenishment and Forecast.

Fleet Dashboard

Where it is: Inventory Central → Fleet Dashboard.

Fleet keeps every vehicle healthy, legal, and honestly used. Everything hinges on one habit: entering the odometer on every fuel fill — that's what powers MPG, mileage-based maintenance, inspection due dates, and the honest-use check.

1.Fleet Dashboard

  1. Read the flags.

    Two counters up top — red (act now) and amber (worth a look) — across the fleet, a Compliance panel (insurance/document expirations, inspections due), and a card per vehicle (odometer, last MPG, cost/gallon, last fill, flags). The flags come from the other Fleet screens, so keeping those current keeps the dashboard honest.

2.Vehicles

  1. Add each vehicle.

    Year, make, model, VIN, plate, color; tank capacity and expected MPG range (these power the fuel flags); purchase/in-service/ownership; home base and fuel-card number (the card number routes imported fills to this truck).

  2. Assign a driver.

    Use Reassign (not the locked field) — it keeps a dated history of who was responsible when. The current driver links the truck to its stocking location in Inventory.

3.Fuel Log

  1. Log every fill.

    Date, odometer, gallons, total cost, station, fuel card. It computes cost/gallon, MPG, and cost/mile, and flags over-tank fills, low/high MPG, or price spikes.

  2. Or import a batch.

    A CSV, or a PDF fuel-card statement (Circle K, RaceTrac…) that Quincy reads into fill rows. Map each card number to its vehicle once and imported fills land on the right truck. Enter the odometer every time — without it MPG and mileage flags can't compute.

4.Maintenance

  1. Create PM tasks.

    An interval by miles, days, or engine hours (oil change every 5,000 mi; wash every 14 days). Set the last-done baseline so the countdown is accurate; complete a task to log a service record and reset the schedule.

  2. Know when they flag.

    Mileage tasks turn amber within 500 miles, time tasks within 7 days, red when overdue — all on the dashboard. Mileage comes from the fuel-log odometer.

5.Repairs & Cost

  1. Log issues and repairs.

    Record an issue with severity (open issues flag on the dashboard); record a repair with labor, parts, vendor, and downtime. Resolving a repair closes its issue. Rolls up total repair cost and downtime per vehicle. (Failed inspection items open issues here automatically.)

6.Routes & GPS — the honest-use check

  1. Compare driven vs explained miles.

    Miles driven (fuel-log odometer) vs miles the tech's driven-to jobs explain (chained home → jobs → home, counting only jobs they pressed "On My Way" for). A red flag when driving is well above what the jobs account for. Needs odometer readings, a home base, and driven-to jobs; GPS breadcrumbs show as supporting context.

7.Inspections

  1. Run the walk-around.

    Pick a vehicle, mark each item pass/fail/N/A with notes; submitting opens a repair issue for every failed item so defects aren't lost.

  2. Customize & schedule.

    "Manage checklist" copies the standard list to add/remove items. "Schedule" sets a cadence by time or miles, whichever comes first; each vehicle shows OK / Due soon / Overdue and due ones flag on the dashboard.

8.Insurance & Documents

  1. Keep vehicles legal.

    Record insurance (carrier, policy #, NAIC, effective/expiration, coverage, agent) covering the whole fleet or specific vehicles, and upload the card as proof (View/Print from the desktop). Track registration, title, DOT/MC, emissions, permits with numbers, expirations, and scans. Expirations flag amber in-window, red once lapsed.

Why it works this way

Proof of insurance prints from the desktop only, to keep it under office control — it's never exposed on the mobile app.
